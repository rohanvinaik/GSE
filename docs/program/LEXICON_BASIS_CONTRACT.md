# GSE lexical basis contract

This is the authored completion of the prime definitions requested in F148 and F158, under the semantic-design delegation at F152. It preserves the existing 72 names, fourteen opposite-pole aliases and 58 canonical signed coordinates. These GSE choices are not attributed to the NSM inventory. The founder record controls intent; the chart and cited primary literature identify NSM lexical units. The research packets locate evidence and do not decide the definitions.

## Meaning, warrants and closure

Each row below identifies a located prime reading. Its complete profile is in `gse/lexicon_builder/foundation.json`: typed identity, own head, arguments, permissions, reference bindings, applicable degree and an authored decision for every coordinate. An ordinary homograph does not inherit this boundary. Existing examined definitions remain unchanged.

The chart presents some combinations. Every entry of each selected chart box is classified as a permission unless it establishes the prime's own degree domain or an explicit prohibition. A participant, topic or co-occurring operator is not automatically a lexical component. The author explicitly declares the remainder silent only after identifying the intended sense and its companions; reconstruction tests consistency, not independent semantic completeness (F158 §3; F159). Removing the declaration reopens that remainder.

Identity, typed head exclusions and degree facts are rebuilt by the shared constructor and bank rules. No expected content or zero vector is injected into its result. MUST's independently sourced CAN and NOT components are input claims; its expected decisions are separate checks. A missing claim or contradictory rule remains observable.

The eight person/thing coordinates record the prime's own head. The established prime-only one-hot convention applies to I, YOU, SOMEONE, SOMETHING, PEOPLE, BODY, KIND and PART. Authored modifier, predicate, operator, time and place head classifications invoke their existing scoped exclusions. WORDS is a linguistic substantive outside those eight coordinates: the eight are silent under its authored boundary, not zeroed through a false operator classification. Ordinary nouns never acquire this prime-only boundary or one-hot restriction.

## Explicit adaptation decisions

The following are local definitions, not claims that the literature proves a GSE extension.

* QUANTITY identifies an amount/cardinality dimension. SOME, MANY, FEW, ALL, MOST, EVERY and related entries retain quantifier identity, count/mass range and logical scope. ALL's universal coverage is not replaced by a numeric rank. No universal amount or total ordering of quantifiers is assumed.
* INFER is a bound evidence-to-conclusion episode. Its conclusion need not be true or known; grouping it with THINK/KNOW is metadata and adds neither component.
* ALIVE/DEAD are vitality states. NSM LIVE/DIE identify the neighboring concepts; the state adaptation removes their aspectual mismatch. DEAD alone asserts neither a dying event nor END.
* THEN is a contextually identified non-now time. It asserts neither pastness nor futurity, and no BEFORE, AFTER or WHEN companion. NOW is the utterance-time pole. The profile declares bindings; quotation and shifted-reference execution require downstream consumer evidence.
* START/END identify event or state boundaries. An event-variable annotation alone is not a lexical boundary, and END does not imply goal completion.
* OUTSIDE/INSIDE are exterior/interior containment poles. A negative source observation is not automatically the opposite pole: not-inside may remain unresolved rather than outside. FALSE is the explicit false truth pole; general negation does not automatically choose it.
* TOWARD/AWAY are oriented relations with a target/source binding. A stationary object can have an orientation. MOVE requires independent evidence.
* WITH/WITHOUT are accompaniment poles in a selected relation. Instrumental, material, manner and causal English WITH readings remain separate construction alternatives; a valency marker writes no accompaniment cell on its governing word.
* MUST retains the agreed practical-necessity adaptation of the chart's “can't not”: `NOT(CAN($bearer, NOT($necessary_act)))`. CAN and NOT occur in its lexical projection. Both negations retain their scopes in the construction tree; they neither cancel nor become duplicate coordinates. DO or HAPPEN of the necessary act is not asserted. Epistemic MUST and weaker SHOULD are not silently treated as this reading.
* CAUSE denotes a brought-about outcome in a distinct affected participant under option B (F144). Its causer can be agentive or event-valued, so neither DO nor HAPPEN is a universal companion of the CAUSE prime. The conditional source adapter may establish one when its participant/event bindings do. BECAUSE remains the explanatory connective; BECAUSE's CAUSE cell stays silent.
* CERTAINTY is an epistemic scale-domain marker over a bound proposition and epistemic holder/context. Its presence is not a claim of certainty, KNOW, TRUE, MAYBE or calibrated probability.

## Hierarchy direction, degree and NUMBER

INTENSITY means degree applicability, as explicitly settled by F125–F126. It is not a threshold for VERY-like strength. The chart's graded primes GOOD, BAD, BIG, SMALL, FAR, NEAR, LONG_TIME, SHORT_TIME and WANT carry INTENSITY through the shared degree law.

HIER_PLUS and HIER_MINUS are separate unpaired coordinates whose written +1 means respectively UP and DOWN on a **bound** QUANTITY, INTENSITY or CERTAINTY scale. The operator alone supplies no base, reference position or amount. BOTH may only occur when distinct scoped operators warrant them; no blanket one-hot assertion over arbitrary composed meanings is introduced here.

GSE_V2 §1.3 used repeated hierarchy markers to represent ordinal position. The current ternary projection preserves direction once, while multiplicity/position belongs in the scoped ordinal or NUMBER record. Counts such as `HIER_PLUS:2` are illegal lexical states. HIER direction is semantic operator content; NUMBER records amount or relative position without adding semantic content. A source's doubled signs can supply a native ordinal observation without writing a HIER coordinate.

NUMBER is an operation, not a 73rd lexical prime. It cannot add/delete a row, change zero, choose an unresolved value or reverse polarity (F098). Positive realization gains preserve the existing mask and sign. Ordinal evidence need not be converted to a numerical gain. The contract does not claim that current NUMBER comparison already calibrates all quantity and certainty scales.

## Function-word homes

Every hand-built function entry explicitly states its operation, required binding, lexical-projection status, alternatives and basis gaps. Construction-only entries have a `CONSTRUCTION_ONLY` account and no fabricated zeros. Entries with sourced components emit partial lexical projections; they do not acquire a prime's complete boundary.

Articles A/AN introduce an indefinite singular reference; singular grammar does not assert ONE. THE requires an identifiable referent and does not assert THIS. Demonstrative THIS/THESE retain the THIS basis gap. IT preserves referential and dummy alternatives: the chart's selected THIS THING reading projects SOMETHING, while the dummy reading stays construction-only. THEY/THEM preserve distinct plural-people, plural-things and singular-person candidates, projecting PEOPLE, SOMETHING or SOMEONE respectively while retaining the THIS gap. None is asserted for every occurrence. WE includes the speaker and keeps addressee inclusion unresolved. Gendered and possessive reference features remain structural.

WELL contributes GOOD in its chart-defined adverbial reading. SUCH contributes LIKE and retains THIS as a gap. EVERYWHERE contributes QUANTITY and WHERE; temporal quantifier portmanteaus contribute QUANTITY and WHEN because the chart explicitly names their components, unlike family metadata. NEVER retains NONE scope over times without inventing a separate NOT component from a chart that does not supply that decomposition. FEW has a degree-domain claim from the chart's VERY FEW construction; it does not acquire an unsupported non-emptiness assertion. A LOT contributes QUANTITY and the applicable degree domain INTENSITY, with its strength retained structurally. These projections are GSE adaptations of the chart's quantifier families, not claims that QUANTITY is an NSM prime.

English IN, AT, OF, ABOUT and instrumental/coagent WITH retain their bound valency operation. FEEL and DO reject ABOUT valency in the chart's prime reading. BE's location and specification frames are retained as distinct construction alternatives despite the shared GSE coordinate; EXIST retains existential THERE IS. HAVE means possession here: auxiliary HAVE and part-whole HAVE are separate constructions. This is an explicit fixed-basis projection loss, not an assertion that NSM merges these primes.

## Intended lexical units

| GSE name | Authored intended sense |
|---|---|
| I | The first-person referent: the speaker in the current speech context. |
| YOU | The addressed person in the current speech context; second-person reference, not a generic person classification. |
| SOMEONE | An unspecified person referent in the prime sense, distinct from a category of persons. |
| SOMETHING | An unspecified thing referent in the prime sense, including the THING allolex. |
| PEOPLE | People as the substantive prime; the plurality of persons is not a separately projected QUANTITY component. |
| BODY | The body as a substantive whole associated with a person; the possessor and the parts are argument relations. |
| KIND | A kind or category itself, rather than an instance merely belonging to a kind. |
| PART | A part in relation to a whole; the whole and its material or bodily type are bound arguments. |
| SAME | Identity of the relevant referent, content, time or place under a comparison binding. |
| OTHER | Distinctness from the relevant referent, content, time or place under that binding; the negative SAME pole. |
| ONE | The cardinal one reading over a scoped counted domain; not the indefinite article or an arbitrary singular noun. |
| TWO | The cardinal two reading over a scoped counted domain; not generic plurality. |
| QUANTITY | An amount or cardinality is the lexical dimension of the selected reading; the quantified domain, quantifier identity and amount are separate bindings. |
| GOOD | The primitive positive evaluator; an applicable degree domain is part of this GSE projection. |
| BAD | The primitive negative evaluator, the negative GOOD pole; an applicable degree domain is part of this GSE projection. |
| BIG | The primitive large-size descriptor; its bearer is an argument and its content has an applicable degree domain. |
| SMALL | The primitive small-size descriptor, the negative BIG pole; its content has an applicable degree domain. |
| THINK | The mental predicate in THINK ABOUT and THINK LIKE THIS frames; its content and topic remain arguments. |
| KNOW | The mental predicate KNOW in its primitive epistemic sense; its knower and known content remain arguments. |
| WANT | The volitional predicate with a wanted thing or event; WANT is degree-bearing, while the wanted act is not its component. |
| FEEL | The unqualified feeling predicate; a bodily locus or good/bad feeling may be supplied, but neither a valence pole nor degree is asserted by FEEL alone. |
| SEE | The visual-perception prime, not understand or infer; perceiver, perceived referent and location remain slots. |
| HEAR | The auditory-perception prime, not obey or learn; perceiver and heard content remain slots. |
| INFER | Draw a conclusion from identified evidence in a bounded inference episode; the conclusion is defeasible and neither KNOW nor truth is entailed. |
| SAY | The primitive saying predicate with content and optional addressee or topic; saying is not itself doing or causing an outcome. |
| WORDS | Linguistic words in the prime sense; these form a linguistic substantive head outside the eight person/thing head coordinates. |
| DO | The primitive action predicate; agent, affected participant, instrument and companion belong to its frame. |
| HAPPEN | The primitive happening predicate, optionally happening to someone or something; agentive action is not required. |
| MOVE | The primitive movement predicate for a person, thing or body part; it does not require a goal, departure, action or causative event. |
| BE | GSE retains one coordinate for the located or specification BE readings; each reading keeps its distinct location or specification frame. |
| HAVE | Possession in the sense of something being someone's; auxiliary HAVE and part-whole HAVE are different constructions. |
| EXIST | Existential THERE IS/ARE in the primitive sense; it is distinct from locative BE and possession HAVE. |
| ALIVE | The positive vitality state of a bearer, abstracted from NSM LIVE; it does not assert a duration, event or activity. |
| DEAD | The negative vitality state of a bearer, abstracted from the result of NSM DIE; it does not assert the death event or a START/END transition. |
| MOMENT | A moment as the primitive punctual temporal unit; quantified or relative-time uses do not add ONE, BEFORE or AFTER to its content. |
| WHEN | The time prime, including its WHEN allolex; the temporal anchor and event occupying it remain construction bindings. |
| LONG_TIME | Primitive long duration, with an applicable degree domain; it does not inherit the separate WHEN coordinate from temporal typing. |
| SHORT_TIME | Primitive short duration, the negative LONG_TIME pole, with an applicable degree domain. |
| BEFORE | Temporal precedence between bound relata; their duration and event content are not components of this relation. |
| AFTER | Temporal succession between bound relata, the negative BEFORE pole; duration is not asserted. |
| THEN | A contextually identified time distinct from the current utterance time, the positive temporal-reference pole; it need not be past, future or subsequent. |
| NOW | The time of the current utterance context, the negative THEN pole; speech itself remains outside its lexical cells. |
| START | The initial boundary of a bound event or state, not a mere start(E) annotation on a source event variable. |
| END | The terminal boundary of a bound event or state, the negative START pole; stopping does not automatically assert completion of a goal. |
| WHERE | The place prime, including WHERE and SOMEWHERE allolexes; location is distinct from a thing located there. |
| HERE | The speaker-centered place in the current utterance context; no WHERE companion follows from spatial typing. |
| SIDE | A spatial side of a bound whole; the whole need not be a body or an object of any particular kind. |
| TOUCH | Primitive contact between two bound referents, without an automatic action, movement or bodily interpretation. |
| ABOVE | The positive vertical or ordered-position relation relative to a bound reference; distance and social rank remain separate readings or bindings. |
| BELOW | The negative ABOVE pole relative to a bound reference; distance is not asserted. |
| FAR | Primitive far distance relative to a bound reference, with an applicable degree domain. |
| NEAR | Primitive near distance, the negative FAR pole, with an applicable degree domain. |
| OUTSIDE | The exterior-containment relation to a bound container, the positive counterpart of INSIDE in GSE; it is not mere negation of being inside. |
| INSIDE | The primitive interior-containment relation to a bound container, represented by negative OUTSIDE in GSE. |
| TOWARD | Orientation toward a bound target; actual movement, approach and arrival are not entailed. |
| AWAY | Orientation away from a bound source, the negative TOWARD pole; actual movement or departure is not entailed. |
| WITH | Accompaniment of a bound referent in the selected GSE relation; instrumental and other English with uses remain separate construction readings. |
| WITHOUT | Excluded accompaniment of a bound referent, the negative WITH pole; absence applies to this relation, not to every use of with. |
| NOT | Negation with an explicit predicate or proposition scope; it is not an instruction to flip every bipolar component of an arbitrary word. |
| MAYBE | Primitive epistemic possibility applied to a proposition; it does not establish ability, knowledge or a calibrated probability. |
| CAN | Primitive possibility or ability in its identified frame; the bearer and possible content remain arguments. |
| MUST | The agreed GSE practical-necessity reading: a bearer cannot not perform the scoped act; CAN and NOT occur in the nested construction, while the act remains a slot. |
| BECAUSE | The explanatory reason relation between propositions, including a thought-to-feeling reason; it is distinct from the lexical causing event. |
| CAUSE | A causer brings about an outcome in a distinct bound participant, whether the causer is a person or an event; agentive DO and event HAPPEN are alternatives, not universal companions. |
| IF | The primitive conditional relation between an antecedent and consequent; neither event is asserted by the connective. |
| LIKE | The primitive similarity or manner relation; its AS and WAY allolexes apply in their documented frames, not the WANT/liking homograph. |
| TRUE | Primitive truth of a bound content; negated truth is a construction rather than a universal antonym-flipping procedure. |
| FALSE | The explicitly false pole of bound propositional content in GSE, represented by negative TRUE; no separate NOT companion is required by the signed alias. |
| HIER_PLUS | An upward direction operator on a bound QUANTITY, INTENSITY or CERTAINTY scale; it contributes direction, not a base, marker count or numeric gain. |
| HIER_MINUS | A downward direction operator on a bound QUANTITY, INTENSITY or CERTAINTY scale; it is its own positive unpaired coordinate, not a negative HIER_PLUS cell. |
| INTENSITY | Applicability of degree-bearing definitional content; it does not by itself assert amplification, VERY, a direction or a numerical magnitude. |
| CERTAINTY | Applicability of an epistemic certainty scale to a proposition; it does not assert that the proposition is certain, true or known. |

## Evidence and limitations

Authority: founder F067, F076, F098, F125–F126, F144, F148, F151–F152 and F158–F160; NSM chart v20 (10 May 2022), including its footer distinction between primes, grammar and portmanteaus. For possession and distinct BE frames see Goddard 2012 Table 1 and note 3, and Goddard & Wierzbicka 2014 Table 1.1. GSE_V2 §1.3 is historical hierarchy evidence; the later founder NUMBER contract governs its current projection. The three 2026-10-25 research packets record source locations and disagreements.

No universal numerical calibration, certainty probability, runtime shifted-deixis resolver or THIS coordinate is invented. The construction metadata preserves those requirements or gaps. Hand-authored prime completeness is an explicit convention checked jointly with the rules; it does not make ordinary lexical source coverage complete.
