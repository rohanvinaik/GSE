# Explicit personal capacity

The saved NSM chart licenses CAN in action frames such as *someone can't do something*. It gives WANT separate frames. A native noun gloss with an explicit person referent and an active capacity construction can identify someone partly by what they can do. In *aliterate*, the capacity to read coexists with a stated disinclination. This projection concerns CAN and supplies no WANT, DO or HAPPEN sign. The English relative clause is evidence about the definition. The projection does not require that the dictionary gloss itself be a well-formed NSM explication. The guard recognizes an exact native noun definition opening with *a/the person* or *someone*, followed by a subject relative and *can* or *is able to* with an active base-form verb. The current frame reserves punctuation that can introduce alternative definitions, disjunction, negated or restricted capacity, and *be*, *get*, or *have* complements. The passive *someone who can be employed* and the mixed-gloss *a person who can read; a literate person* remain outside the frame. The rule also requires an independently proved SOMEONE=+1. This keeps the Wiktionary *aliterate* target open on this baseline while the exact WordNet *aliterate* sense is eligible.

The frame can coexist with the earlier coordinated-person frame. The Wiktionary *literate* sense requires that overlap. The audits use the Wiktionary *literate* sense and the WordNet *aliterate* and *signer* senses, with separate release checks on *bilingual*, *guide*, and the WordNet *literate* sense.
