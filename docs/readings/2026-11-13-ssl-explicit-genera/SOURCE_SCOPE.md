# Explicit person, place, and time genera

Release 011 provides the baseline. The rule families use complete native noun glosses with exact WordNet or Wiktionary identity. They identify the genus of the defined sense. They leave the type of the modifying action, state, or participant to separately warranted rules. [Prior capacity rule](../2026-11-13-ssl-person-capacity/SOURCE_SCOPE.md) · [Saved NSM chart](../../../data/external/references/nsm-primes-chart-v20-2022.txt)

- The person rule extends the audited coordinated-person scope to a single genus introduced by *a/the person* or *someone* and a subject relative. Negation in the relative can restrict the description while preserving the person genus. Definitions such as *a person who is part mortal and part god* are reserved for review of their mixed ontology. Distinct senses of the same lemma retain separate domains. The held-out senses test a person who receives a vaccine and a person who drives racing cars.
- The place rule covers *a place where* and *a place in which*. The NSM WHERE prime explicitly permits the place of an event. The location can be defined by what is kept there, what happens there, or who meets there. The scope reserves the definite form for a later review of conventional, imagined, and metaphorical locations. The held-out senses test a place where bees are kept and a place where people meet.
- The time rule covers *a/the time* and *a/the period of time* followed by *when* or *during which*. Both points and intervals can identify a WHEN coordinate. The rule leaves extent, duration attachment, and the referenced event to their own evidence. The held-out senses test the onset of daylight and the interval in which a contract is effective.

The experiment forecasts 355 SOMEONE, 68 WHERE, and 35 WHEN assignments. On the exact Wiktionary *aliterate* sense, the person rule also supplies the missing premise of the admitted CAN rule. That interaction adds one CAN assignment to the joint forecast. The release checks reserve competing genera and homographs, and they distinguish source records from distinct native sense groups.
