# Discovery from exact noun definitions across sources

The comparison uses exact WordNet and Wiktionary senses whose native glosses supply a shared definitional construction. The census retains native source identity, the complete gloss, part of speech, existing domains, and the SUBTLEX spelling count. The spelling count orders candidate questions. It supplies no signed lexical evidence.

- The explicit partitive construction identifies something by its being a part of a whole. The reviewed records include parts of objects, materials, bodies, places, periods, and statements. The lexical warrant supplies PART=+1. The whole remains a contextual referent. The closure curriculum forecasts the open coordinate on the spatial sense of *back*. Its withheld senses are the key component *bit* in WordNet and the photographic-system component *stop* in Wiktionary.
- The person-relative construction keeps an explicit person as the genus while the relative clause supplies coordinated material. The attachment test reserves separate glosses, alternate genera, and nominal coordination at the second genus. The proposed sign is SOMEONE=+1. The spelling-frequency curriculum selects the playground-tag sense of *it*. The held-out senses are the WordNet person canonized as a *saint* and the Wiktionary person who runs a multi-user dungeon, a sense of *god*.
- The explicit word construction identifies a singular word by its use, meaning, or function. Its scope reserves definitions that allow a phrase, sign, or another alternative genus. This construction supplies a proposal for WORDS=+1. Its held-out senses are the WordNet exclamation sense of *devil* and the Wiktionary negation-word sense of *negative*.
- The partitive SOMETHING proposal asks whether PART=+1 can supply a premise for the noun's own entity reference within the same scope. Its forecast distinguishes independently resolved cells from cells that become reachable only when both rules apply. This proposal has a separate teaching and audit obligation.

Both curricula start from the validated release after the right-hand round. Each receives one typed teaching question and two held-out questions, using the same frozen inventory and model. For a direct scope proposal, one audit comes from each native source. A proposal needs a source warrant, a teaching agreement, two audit agreements, exact delta comparison, and full replay before its arm counts as validated. The two arms have the same three-question budget. Potential is the sum of log₂ domain cardinality over lexical records. The comparison records both absolute reduction and reduction as a fraction of the common initial residual. This experiment measures one step in the curriculum and keeps projected joint gain distinct from realized contraction.

The exploration on 005 found 264 exact partitive definitions, 68 person definitions, and 24 word definitions. It forecast 228 PART, 29 SOMEONE, and 24 WORDS assignments. The joint forecast contained 163 additional SOMETHING assignments beyond the isolated effects. The production comparison regenerates these forecasts from the successor. ## Measured on release 006

Both arms used one teaching question and two held-out audits. Both rules were admitted and replayed all 401,596 records with complete support search and zero mismatches. Neither arm reopened a domain.

Closure removed 7.86 times as much unweighted potential. Frequency alone removed 2.73 times as much under SUBTLEX weighting. The publication candidate is chosen by unweighted corpus contraction. The weighted statistic divides each spelling's corpus count equally among its lexical records, so the result measures spelling-weighted contraction. Both gains are small fractions of the starting 35,000,666.67 bits. The 24 WORDS and 163 joint-only SOMETHING assignments remain forecasts.
